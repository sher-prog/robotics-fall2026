# mission_2

## comparison
In the normal condition, the classical detector had 0 correct boxes and 2 missed mugs, while the learned detector had 2 correct boxes and 0 missed mugs. The classical detector completely failed in the dim and occluded conditions, getting 2 missed mugs in both. In contrast, the learned detector maintained 1 correct box in dim lighting. Furthermore, while both had 2 misses in the occluded condition, the classical detector generated 2 false boxes compared to the learned detector's 0 false boxes. This difference is critical because real-world environments change. For example, in dim lighting, the classical detector misses the mug entirely because the shadow makes it too dark for a strict color rule, but the learned detector is still able to recognize it.

## choice
I recommend the learned detector at an acceptance threshold of 0.30 because it is far more reliable when lighting changes or the mug is partially blocked. Although the classical detector processes images faster at around 7 to 9 milliseconds per image compared to the learned detector's 49 milliseconds, the learned detector's superior accuracy makes it the better choice. A foreground mask alone cannot identify the mug because it simply highlights everything in the front (like the laptop and keyboard) without labeling which specific object is the cup. A major limitation of this test is that we only evaluated a small number of images on a single desk (3 images per condition), so we do not know how the model will perform in a brand-new environment with different backgrounds.
