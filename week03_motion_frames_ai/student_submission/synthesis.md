# Technical synthesis

Even though my L-path program is technically correct and successfully meets all distance and angle requirements in simulation, it assumes an empty, unchanging environment. In a real shared hallway, pedestrian such as someone using a wheelchair, navigating with a cane, or simply looking down at a phone, will not move out of the way like a simulated world allows. Because the robot lacks obstacle detection, it cannot slow down, reroute, or signal its intentions if a person steps into its path. To evaluate whether this system is truly safe for deployment, relying solely on simulation logs is not enough. We would need additional evidence from real-world hallway trials, live sensor data on obstacle detection, and direct observations of how people actually respond to the robot in shared spaces.
