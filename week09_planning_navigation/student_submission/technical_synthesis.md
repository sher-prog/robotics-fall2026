A route that suits a robot sharing space with people has to be short, finish reliably and keep a safe distance from people. In Mission 1, BFS and A* both found the shortest open-map path (13.5 m), but A* needed fewer expansions (700 vs 928). DFS found a path that was far too long (111 m). In Mission 2 that long path mattered, the DFS timed out on both maps. On the constrained map, Nav2's smoothed route was faster (73.3 s) than BFS (99.7 s, controller_failed). So a good route is short and easy to drive,  which is not just valid on a grid. In Mission 3, the social fields added space at almost no cost. RRT social_2 kept 1.71 m clearance in 43.7 s, while A* social_2 kept only 0.73 m. But these fields can fail ao A* social_1 ended in field_oscillation and never moved. I'd choose the route that is reliable, reasonably fast and keeps the most space. Mission 1 numbers are exact on the grid. Clearance, distance and time in Missions 2 and 3 are estimates from the robot's pose, not ground truth. I only ran one trial per cell, and the people were fixed, so this doesn't prove how the robot behaves around people who move.
