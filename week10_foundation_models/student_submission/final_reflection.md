Going through this lab was honestly a huge reality check. I use AI for coding, bug checks and to explain concepts to me all the time, I usually think of these models as incredibly capable. But seeing how easily the model confidently hallucinated robot movements, like trying to pick up a cup it physically couldn’t, made me more aware of how completely different software intelligence is from physical robotics.
In Mission 3, the robot just kept looping up and down in a simple grid. It was frustrating but also eye-opening to watch. It showed me that just because a model can write complex text doesn't mean it has basic spatial awareness or common sense.  It’s easy to get caught up in the hype of foundation models, but this activity highlighted the massive safety risks of deploying them in the real world without strict guardrails and action checks. If an AI hallucinates in a chat box, you get a weird answer; if it hallucinates while driving a physical robot, it could break something or hurt someone. It really motivated me to focus on the intersection of AI development and safety, ensuring we build systems that actually understand their physical constraints before we trust them.
