Throughout the three missions in this lab, I learned that translating an AI model's text-based responses into useful, physical robot behavior requires highly constrained, specific prompts rather than open-ended commands. Left to their own devices, large language models will often hallucinate capabilities the robot does not physically possess.
In Mission 1, refining my request to explicitly match the robot's actual action capabilities (moving its wheels and using its camera) prevented it from attempting impossible tasks like picking up or carrying objects. In Mission 2, I saw how crucial visible evidence and action checks are. By instructing the model to separate what it could visibly confirm in the image from what it assumed, I stopped it from inventing fake physical distances and world coordinates that could cause a robot to crash or malfunction in the real world.
However, Mission 3 highlighted a significant remaining limitation where current language models lack true spatial reasoning and working memory for physical navigation. Even when provided with strict, step-by-step conditional logic based on grid coordinates, the robot became trapped in an endless loop of moving up and down. It could not dynamically understand the maze's wall layout or remember its past failed moves to correct its path. This demonstrates that while models are excellent at generating plausible text plans, they cannot safely govern real-time physical navigation without external, specialized spatial systems and strict guardrails guiding them.
